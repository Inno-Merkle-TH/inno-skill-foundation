# 01 — Quality Engineering and Delivery

## Outcomes

Turn product uncertainty into actionable quality work.

## Prerequisites

None. Use a local document; no accounts or services are needed.

Commands start at the repository root unless a different directory is named. Use a fresh root terminal for a new block; never guess your working directory.

## Concepts

Quality is shared by product, engineering and operations. QE makes risks visible and builds feedback into design, implementation and operation. A test result is evidence about a particular version, not permission to release by itself.

## Worked Example

A story says: “Customers can buy a notebook.” Before automation, ask what completed means, which currencies are supported, and what happens if tracking fails.

## Guided Lab

1. Create a synthetic sprint board with the story, acceptance questions, test work and release risks.
2. Assign product decisions to a product owner and technical decisions to an engineer; record where QE contributes.
3. Write a refinement note, a concise daily risk update and one measurable retrospective action.

## Expected Results

Three distinct artifacts describe decisions and next actions, not ceremony attendance.

## Independent Challenge

Change the delivery date without changing scope. Explain which risks need a decision rather than silently reducing coverage.

## Troubleshooting

If the board only lists test execution, add requirement review, testability and monitoring work.

## Completion Checklist

- [ ] Recorded decision owners and unresolved questions.
- [ ] Connected a retrospective action to an observable improvement.
- [ ] Saved expected/actual results and execution limits in [learning evidence](../../templates/learning-evidence.md).

## Understanding Checklist

- [ ] Explain why quality is not owned by QA alone.
- [ ] Distinguish test evidence from risk acceptance.

## Cleanup and Handoff
